01 · What it is

Dogs riding waves. Really.

Ridden for fun, for charity and in competition

  • Simple definition: a dog rides a surfboard on a breaking wave, usually in small surf close to shore.
  • Solo: the dog rides alone. A handler in the water lines the board up and pushes it into the wave.
  • Tandem: dog and human share one board.
  • Group: several dogs on one board, sometimes with a person too.
  • Most dog surfing is just fun at the beach. Organised events are often charity fundraisers, and some are serious competitions.

03 · The surfers

Any dog that loves water

  • Any size can surf
  • Stocky builds balance well
  • Must swim with confidence
  • Flat faces need extra care

Contests group dogs by size

  • There's no single surfing breed. Small terriers and big retrievers both do it.
  • Low, stocky dogs often find balance easier, which is why bulldogs and corgis are crowd favourites.
  • Non-negotiable: the dog already enjoys water and swims confidently before it ever sees a board.
  • Flat-faced breeds like bulldogs and pugs tire and overheat faster and are weaker swimmers. Life jacket always, sessions short.
  • Contests usually split dogs into size classes so small dogs aren't judged against giants.
  • ⚑ Verify: size bands and weight cut-offs differ between events.

04 · Gear

The kit

Dog life jacketWith a grab handle on the back
Soft-top boardWide, stable, with a grippy deck
Fresh water & shadeSo the dog isn’t drinking the sea
Dog-safe sunscreenFor pale noses and thin coats
  • Life jacket: made for dogs, brightly coloured, with a grab handle on the back so you can lift the dog out of the water fast.
  • Board: big, wide soft-top boards are stable and forgiving when the dog falls. Paws need grip, so a traction pad or non-slip deck helps.
  • Fresh water and shade: dogs swallow seawater while surfing, and too much salt water can make them ill. Offer fresh water often and rest in the shade.
  • Sunscreen: pink noses and thin coats burn. Use a dog-safe product; not every human sunscreen is safe if licked.
  • ⚑ Verify: sunscreen guidance with a vet before recommending a product.

05 · Training

Sand first, surf later

1

Jacket on at home

2

Board on grass or sand

3

Calm, shallow water

4

Tiny waves, with a catcher

5

Grow at the dog’s pace

  • Dogs learn this the way they learn anything: tiny steps, lots of rewards, short sessions.
  • 1. Life jacket on at home, with treats, until the dog stops noticing it.
  • 2. Board on grass or sand: reward stepping on, then standing, then staying while you gently rock it.
  • 3. Calm, shallow water: you hold the board steady while the dog stands.
  • 4. Tiny whitewater: the handler pushes the board into a small wave, and a catcher waits near the shore.
  • 5. Move on only when the dog chooses to climb back on. If it hesitates or jumps off, go back a step. End on a good ride.

06 · Competition

Judged on the ride

Judges typically look at

  • Confidence on the board
  • Length of the ride
  • Size of the wave
  • Style, and the odd trick

Divisions

By dog size Tandem Multi-dog
  • Contests usually run in short heats. Each dog gets a set time to catch waves, and the best rides count.
  • Judges commonly look at confidence, ride length and wave size. Style and tricks win the crowd.
  • Divisions are typically by dog size, plus tandem (dog and human) and multi-dog classes.
  • ⚑ Verify: heat format, criteria and scoring vary by event.
  • Examples: the World Dog Surfing Championships in Pacifica, California, and Surf City Surf Dog in Huntington Beach.

07 · Safety

The dog decides

Keep going

  • Loose, wiggly body
  • Climbs back on by itself
  • Soft, relaxed face

Stop

  • Tail tucked
  • Trying to jump off
  • Shaking or wide eyes
Small wavesNo ripsShort sessionsRinse off after
  • The golden rule: the dog has to want this. Never place or hold a reluctant dog on a board.
  • Keep going when you see a loose, wiggly body, a soft face, and the dog climbing back on by itself.
  • Stop when you see a tucked tail, attempts to jump off, shaking, lip licking or the whites of the eyes.
  • Pick small, clean waves. Avoid rip currents and crowded breaks, and watch for cold and heat.
  • Afterwards, rinse off the salt and sand, dry the ears and check the paw pads.

08 · Why it matters

Why it’s worth it

  • A bond built on trust
  • Exercise and confidence
  • Events that help animals

Surf’s up!

  • It builds trust: the dog learns you'll keep it safe somewhere new and strange.
  • It's exercise and a confidence boost: a new challenge, outdoors, together.
  • It gives back: events raise money for animal welfare, and surf-therapy dogs ride with people with disabilities.
  • ⚑ Verify: Ricochet, a golden retriever from San Diego known as a "SURFice dog", is the best-known example.
  • Close: next time you see a dog in a life jacket paddling out, that's an athlete. Surf's up. Thank you.
